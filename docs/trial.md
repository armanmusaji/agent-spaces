# The trial

Before building the prototype, Claude and GPT Astra worked the fictional task themselves in a real Figma file, with Arman ruling between rounds. This page is the short chronology. Node IDs, thread records and the full log live in the private project folder.

## The task

Harbor Street Library is a fictional public library. Its mobile app needs an event signup flow in three screens: event detail, choose a session, confirm. Each agent produced one direction. Content was fixed so the directions could be compared: one event, three sessions, one of them full.

Two kinds of shared material were kept apart: a read-only reference library of type, color and base components, and a small page-local kit (a Session row component, a content sheet, a phone frame template) that could change only through an explicit exception.

## Ground rules

Every top-level object carries its author's name. Nobody deletes another agent's object; it is set aside instead. During review, agents propose and never apply. After Arman rules, the ruling says what carries forward and who edits next. One writer at a time in Figma.

## Round 1: separate spaces, proposals only (2026-09-25 to 26)

Claude built Direction A (detail page with a sheet for choosing a session). Astra built Direction B (a clear step per page). Each reviewed the other: seven proposals on B, six on A, each pointing at a named object.

Arman opened the file to rule and could not. Every comment showed his name, notes were tiny at the zoom where the page fit, one legend served three screens, and there was no orientation. Over two days the review format changed four times, ending in v1.4: an orientation card, one legend under each screen, callouts in a lane beside the screen, dark note surfaces in distinct fonts, author colors, and a rule never to delete an object someone has commented on. Some of Arman's comments went missing during that rework; frames Claude had deleted were the suspected cause, not confirmed.

Once the surface was readable, Arman's ruling took one exchange: both directions carry forward with the agreed fixes, the step count starts at signup, the fictional product confirms by email and allows cancellation, and the shared Session row master may be repaired once.

## Round 2: shared space, bounded direct edits (2026-09-27)

Both directions moved into one shared section. Astra, named to edit next, made the authorized repair to the shared Session row (removing a stray white fill on three variants; the change is attributed and reversible) and made one direct edit to Claude's receipt, adding the library name, with both names kept on the object. Claude accepted it. Review produced two proposals each way, down from seven and six. Arman ruled from three direct links in under five minutes of attention: a placeholder slightly lighter (Claude's small change over Astra's decline), two wording cuts, and one agreed removal.

## The exception: what actually happened versus what the prototype shows

In the trial, neither direction needed a waitlist, so no waitlist request was made. The one authorized change to a shared component was the white-fill repair above, ruled "allow once". The prototype uses a waitlist request on the same component to illustrate the boundary, because it shows the reach of a change more clearly. That is a demonstration, not a trial event.

## What the trial cannot tell you

Round 2 changed several things at once: the space became shared, cross-edits were allowed, the agents had learned from round 1, and the review format had improved. The trial cannot say which change caused which outcome. Two agents, one task, two rounds. First-hand evidence for the design, not proof.
